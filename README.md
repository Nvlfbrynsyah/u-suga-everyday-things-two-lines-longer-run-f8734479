An entry for the "Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run" competition: two sentences that explain one ordinary thing to a ten-year-old. This entry picks a traffic jam and asks the reader to flick a rug, so the ripple that runs off one end becomes the stop that runs backward through a queue of cars — each driver touching the brake a heartbeat later than the one in front — so that the wave can reach you long after the car that caused it has driven away. The second sentence earns its place by being honest about the analogy rather than praising it: a ripple on a rug fades as it spreads and can slide sideways off the edge, while a jam on a road with no side lanes to spill into can keep its shape or even grow, so the rug gives you the timing and the shape of the problem but not its whole story.
